Republic Act No. 11261

First-Time Jobseekers in Legazpi City

If you are looking for your first job, a national law lets you get most of your pre-employment documents without paying the government fee. You claim it with one piece of paper from your barangay, and you can use it once, within one year.

What the law actually gives you

The First Time Jobseekers Assistance Act waives the government's fees on the documents employers ask for. It does not waive private costs, and it is not a job placement programme.

The fee is waived, not the process

You still apply, queue, and submit requirements like anyone else. What you do not do is pay the government's charge for the document.

Once, and once only

Section 3 of the Act says the benefit “shall only be availed of once.” Agencies keep a roster of who has claimed it.

One year to use it

The implementing rules give you one year from the date your Barangay Certification was issued. After that it lapses.

Are you a first-time jobseeker?

The implementing rules define one as “a Filipino citizen, who is actively seeking employment, locally or abroad, for the first time, as certified by the barangay of which he/she is a resident.” Your barangay can only issue the certification if you have lived there for at least six months.

Past work does not always disqualify you. The rules exclude entrepreneurs and business owners, independent contractors, and beneficiaries of government programmes such as GIP and SPES from counting as “employment” — so having been an SPES student worker does not, by itself, stop you from being a first-time jobseeker.

What is free, and what still costs money

Section 4 of the Act lists what is waived. Section 8 lists what is deliberately left out. Both lists are short enough to read in full.

Fees waived (Section 4)

  • Police clearance certificate
  • National Bureau of Investigation (NBI) clearance
  • Barangay clearance
  • Medical certificate from a public hospital
  • Birth certificate
  • Marriage certificate
  • Transcript of academic records from state colleges and universities
  • Tax Identification Number (TIN)
  • Unified Multi-Purpose ID (UMID) card
  • Other documentary requirements issued by the government that an employer may require of a job applicant

That last line is a catch-all written into the law. If an office refuses a document that an employer genuinely asked for, it is worth pointing to it.

Still payable (Section 8)

  • Professional licensure examinations at the PRC
  • Philippine passport, and DFA authentication of documents
  • Civil Service Examination applications at the CSC
  • Driver's licence applications at the LTO

One more carve-out sits inside the waiver itself: a medical certificate from a public hospital is free, but the laboratory tests and medical procedures behind it are not.

How to claim it, in three steps

Everything starts at your barangay. No other office can certify that you are a first-time jobseeker.

  1. 1

    Go to your barangay hall and ask for the Barangay Certification

    It is a document “issued and signed by the Punong Barangay or his/her authorized officer” stating that you have lived in the barangay for at least six months and are a first-time jobseeker. You will be asked to fill in a short personal information sheet.

    Find your barangay hall and its officials

  2. 2

    Sign the Oath of Undertaking in front of the Punong Barangay

    This is where you commit to the conditions attached to the benefit — chiefly that you are claiming it for the first time. It is executed before the Punong Barangay or an authorised officer. DOLE's own form is headed “this form need not be notarized”, so nobody should be sending you to a notary or charging you for one.

  3. 3

    Present the certification at each office you request a document from

    Bring it every time. Offices usually keep a copy and record your claim, because the law requires them to maintain a roster of everyone issued a document under it. You have one year from the date on the certification.

Which office issues which document

Only some of these are the City Government's to give. The rest are your barangay's or a national agency's, and this page is honest about which is which.

Where to go in Legazpi City

Section 7 of the Act names the Public Employment Service Office as the office that “shall assist first time jobseekers in securing required pre-employment documents.” In Legazpi, that is PESO.

PESO Legazpi

Public Employment Service Office. Beyond the documents, it registers jobseekers, holds vacancies from partner employers, and runs SPES, JobStart and the DOLE livelihood programmes.

Frequently asked questions

Answers here come from the Act and its implementing rules. Where the rules are silent, this page says so instead of guessing.

I worked as an SPES student or a GIP intern. Am I still a first-time jobseeker?

Yes. The implementing rules define “employment” so that beneficiaries of government programmes such as the Government Internship Program and the Special Program for Employment of Students do not count as having been employed. Your barangay's form may ask whether you were a JobStart beneficiary — answer it honestly.

Does the barangay charge for the certification?

Barangay clearance is on the list of documents whose fees are waived. If you are asked to pay for the certification itself, raise it with the Punong Barangay before paying.

What if I lose the certification, or the year runs out?

The one-year clock runs from the date of issuance, and the benefit itself is a once-only entitlement — a fresh certification does not reset a claim you have already used. Ask your barangay what it can reissue, and PESO what your remaining options are.

My school is private. Is my transcript free?

No. The Act covers transcripts “issued by state colleges and universities”. A private school's fees are its own to set.

I need a medical certificate. Is everything free?

The certificate from a public hospital is free. The laboratory tests and medical procedures required before it can be issued are explicitly excluded, so budget for those.

What happens if someone fakes the certification?

The Act treats fabricating the Barangay Certification as an offence punishable under the Revised Penal Code. It is not worth it.
