# History of Legazpi City (Concise)

## Disclaimer

This document was compiled from two publicly accessible sources — the Legazpi City Government's official website and the Wikipedia article on Legazpi, Albay — for general informational and reference purposes only. It is not an official publication of the City Government of Legazpi. For authoritative, current, or official information, please approach the **City Government of Legazpi** directly.

---

## Quick Facts

- **Original name:** Sawangan (now Legazpi Port), a fishing and farming village
- **Renamed "Legazpi":** by Royal Decree, September 22, 1856, honoring conquistador Miguel López de Legazpi
- **First cityhood:** 1892, under the Becerra Law
- **Current charter:** Republic Act No. 2234 (June 12, 1959), amended by RA 5525
- **Population:** roughly 209,500–210,600 depending on the census year cited
- **Tagline:** "City of Fun and Adventure"
- **City seal:** features Mayon Volcano, a ship's wheel/wing/anchor, and bundles of palay, abaca, and coconut

## The Story, in Brief

Legazpi began as **Sawangan**, a small fishing and farming settlement on Albay Gulf, first placed under Franciscan missionary care in 1587. By 1616 it had grown into the Pueblo de Albay, capital of a large colonial district called the Partido de Ibalón. The town endured pirate raids, fires, and natural disasters over the next two centuries.

The most transformative event came on **February 1, 1814**, when Mayon Volcano erupted catastrophically, destroying Sawangan and killing roughly 1,200 people. Survivors scattered — some to the uplands, some to a new inland settlement called Albay Nuevo, and others back to the coast, where they founded **Binanuahan**. In 1856, colonial authorities carved Binanuahan and three neighboring barrios (Lamba, Rawis, and Bigaa) into a new town, officially named **Legazpi** by decree on September 22, 1856, in honor of the Spanish conquistador Miguel López de Legazpi. Don Lorenzo Hao became its first gobernadorcillo.

Legazpi's port grew steadily through the 19th century, becoming an official port of entry in 1872 and one of the busier ports in the Philippines outside Manila. In 1892, it achieved **city status for the first time** under the Becerra Law, merging with Albay and Daraga — though the Philippine Revolution ended Spanish rule just six years later, and the Americans later dissolved this city status in 1908.

Legazpi saw fighting during the Philippine–American War (1900) and, decades later, was occupied by Japanese forces from December 1941 until its liberation by American troops on April 1–2, 1945. After the war, cityhood was restored (1948), dissolved again (1954), and finally re-established for good in **1959** under Republic Act No. 2234 — the charter still in effect today.

Through the late 20th century, Legazpi solidified its role as the Bicol Region's urban and administrative center, becoming the designated regional capital in the 1970s, while repeatedly weathering Mayon eruptions and destructive typhoons (notably Trix in 1952, Sening in 1970, and Reming in 2006, whose lahars buried Barangay Padang). In recent decades, the city has grown into a modern commercial and tourism hub, with major infrastructure like the Embarcadero de Legazpi, the Legazpi Grand Central Terminal, and the Bicol International Airport (2021), alongside national recognition as one of the country's most business-friendly and competitive cities.

## A Note on Discrepancies Between the Two Sources

The city government's account states that a 1973 decree (PD 125) made Daraga part of Legazpi City, but Daraga functions today — and is described elsewhere — as its own separate, neighboring municipality. Minor differences also exist in the exact founding date (September 22 vs. 25 vs. October 23, 1856), the founding barangays credited, and population/land-area figures, largely due to differing reference years between the two sources.
