## 1. Pre-Hispanic and Early Settlement

Human occupation of the wider Albay Gulf area is documented by archaeological finds extending back at least 4,000 years. The earliest securely dated artifact, the Mataas Shell Scoop, was radiocarbon-dated to between roughly 2000 BCE and 300 CE and was declared a National Cultural Treasure in 2010.

Jar-burial complexes at Hoyop-Hoyopan Cave in nearby Camalig, along with comparable sites along the Albay–Sorsogon coast, point to continuous habitation and participation in regional maritime trade networks from the Philippine Early Metal Age (c. 100–500 BCE) through the Song–Ming ceramic trade era. Sixteenth-century Spanish chronicler Miguel de Loarca described the pre-contact coast as densely populated and prosperous, rich in irrigated rice, gold ornaments, and imported silk.

The area began as a barangay called **Sawangan** — a cluster of fishing and farming hamlets along the mangrove flats on the western shore of Albay Gulf.

---

## 2. Spanish Contact and the Mission Era (1573–1750s)

The first Europeans to reach the gulf were Captain Luis Enríquez de Guzmán and Augustinian friar Alonso Jiménez, part of an expedition dispatched by Adelantado Miguel López de Legazpi. In July 1573, a second force under Juan de Salcedo (Legazpi's grandson) entered the peninsula, eventually reaching the village of Albaybay — later shortened to **Albay**.

From 1587 to 1616, spiritual administration of the town was under the Franciscan friars of the **Doctrina of Cagsawa** (now Daraga). In 1587, the Franciscans established a mission chapel in Sawangan as a visita of the Cagsawa parish; Fray Francisco de Santa Ana built the first wooden chapel, dedicated to St. Gregory the Great, known as the Misión de San Gregorio Magno de Sawangan. Sawangan was elevated to a visita regular (full mission station) in 1605, and in 1616 became the independent Pueblo de Albay — capital of the newly created **Partido de Ibalón**, which then encompassed present-day Albay, Sorsogon, Masbate, parts of Camarines Sur, and the islands of Catanduanes, Ticao, and Burias.

Throughout the 17th–18th centuries, the settlement grew through farming, and its sheltered harbor served as an anchorage on the Manila–Acapulco galleon route. Growth was periodically disrupted by raids from Mindanao/Sulu and Dutch privateers; a major fire destroyed the town church in 1754 amid renewed pirate attacks. The community also survived a devastating typhoon in 1742 and a strong earthquake in 1811.

---

## 3. The 1814 Mayon Eruption and Refounding

On February 1, 1814, Mayon Volcano erupted in the most cataclysmic explosion in its recorded history, burying or obliterating nearby settlements — including the church of Cagsawa and the villages of Budiao and Camalig — and killing about 1,200 people, making it Mayon's deadliest known eruption. Sawangan itself was largely leveled.

Survivors, led by parish priest Fray Pedro Licup, first relocated to Makalaya on the slopes of Mount Bariw (present-day Barangay Taysan), but many lowlanders found upland life uncomfortable. Some resettled at **Taytay** (now Barangay Bagumbayan), while others returned to the original Sawangan site and founded **Binanuahan** ("old town"). Because an 1829 Spanish decree barred new towns without government sanction, the inland Taytay settlement (called **Albay Nuevo** or "New Albay") and the coastal Binanuahan were administered as a single town, with Binanuahan as a subordinate visita — a source of local resentment.

In the 1830s–40s both sides rebuilt their churches: a grand stone church at Albay Nuevo (begun 1839, completed 1840s) became today's **St. Gregory the Great Cathedral**, while Binanuahan built a waterfront chapel to a new patron, **St. Raphael the Archangel** (1834) — today's St. Raphael the Archangel Parish Church in the Port District.

---

## 4. The Naming and Founding of Legazpi (1856)

Officially, the name **Legazpi** was given to the ancient village of Sawangan by Royal Decree dated **September 22, 1856**, to perpetuate the memory of the Adelantado Don Miguel Lopez de Legazpi. On July 17, 1856, Governor-General Ramon Montero had decreed that the coastal visita of Binanuahan, together with the barrios of Lamba, Rawis, and Bigaa, be carved out from Albay Nuevo into a new township initially called **Pueblo Viejo** ("Old Town"); the subsequent September decree conferred the name Legazpi, and the town was formally inaugurated on **October 23, 1856**.

**Don Lorenzo Hao** was the first elected Gobernadorcillo, and **Rev. Camilo Abainza** served as acting Parish Priest. The remaining inland settlement kept the name "Albay" (later Albay Nuevo/Albay Viejo) and continued as the provincial capital — corresponding to today's Old Albay District of Legazpi City.

---

## 5. Port, Trade, and First Cityhood (1872–1898)

On May 18, 1872, a royal decree designated Legazpi an official port of entry for foreign trade; this was implemented locally on December 3, 1874, opening the port to direct overseas trade in abacá and copra. By the late 19th century Legazpi was one of the busier ports in the archipelago outside Manila.

Legazpi became **a city for the first time under the Becerra Law of 1892**, which merged Legazpi Port, Albay (Bagumbayan), and the outlying town of Daraga into a single city administration, the **Ayuntamiento of Legazpi (Albay)**, formally established in 1894. The merger with Daraga was unpopular among many Daraga residents.

As the Philippine Revolution spread in 1898, Spanish rule collapsed in Albay; a revolutionary junta under Don Anacieto Solano took over, followed by General Vicente Lukbán, who established the Philippine Republic's authority in Legazpi and Albay by late 1898 — ending over 300 years of Spanish rule.

---

## 6. American Period (1900–1941)

On January 23, 1900, U.S. forces under Brig.-Gen. William A. Kobbe landed at Legazpi to reopen the abacá port. About 800 Bicolano revolutionary troops under Gen. José Ignacio Paua and Col. Antero Reyes fought a day-long action at San Rafael Bridge — regarded as the bloodiest single engagement of the Philippine–American War in Bicol, with 172 Filipino dead. A granite obelisk (the Battle of Legazpi Pylon) marks the site today.

The U.S. military government dissolved the Spanish-era ayuntamiento and, in 1908, reorganized the area into two separate municipalities: **Legazpi Port** (coastal commercial quarter) and **Albay** (inland district, provincial capital) — effectively dissolving the 1892 city into municipal status. Daraga regained its independence in 1922.

Education expanded rapidly: Albay High School opened in 1906, the Albay Normal School in 1921 (both later folded into Bicol University), and St. Agnes Academy was founded in 1912 by the Missionary Benedictine Sisters of Tutzing. Commercial growth included a 1915 electric-light franchise and record abacá exports in 1928–29. Rail (the "Bicol Express") reached Legazpi in 1938, and a pre-war civilian airfield (Sanborn Field) operated from before 1936.

---

## 7. World War II (1941–1945)

At dawn on December 12, 1941, Japanese forces landed at Legazpi without opposition, quickly seizing the airfield and rail line. American air raids that afternoon destroyed nine Japanese aircraft but could not dislodge the garrison. Japanese control rarely extended far beyond the coast and rail line; Bicolano guerrillas under Lt. Col. Demetrio Camua harassed supply lines from the interior.

From October 1944, Allied air raids and, from March 1945, naval bombardment devastated the port district. On April 1, 1945, the U.S. 158th Regimental Combat Team landed at Rawis Beach and secured the town within two days, aided by Camua's guerrillas. Many 19th-century structures, including the original St. Raphael Church and the Academia de Santa Ines, were destroyed and later rebuilt.

---

## 8. Post-War Cityhood, Dissolution, and Restoration (1945–1959)

Commercial air travel resumed in 1946 with Philippine Airlines flights to Manila. On July 18, 1948, Legazpi became a chartered city for the second time by virtue of **Republic Act No. 306**, which merged Legazpi and Daraga, with President Elpidio Quirino appointing Jose R. Arboleda as first mayor. This cityhood was short-lived: **Republic Act No. 993** dissolved it on June 8, 1954, reverting Legazpi and Daraga to separate towns.

During this municipal period, **Typhoon Trix** (October 1952) struck with winds over 220 km/h, virtually destroying the city.

**Republic Act No. 2234**, approved June 12, 1959, re-established Legazpi as a city for the third time and remains the foundation of its charter today; **Republic Act No. 5525** later amended the charter. Mayor Ramon A. Arnaldo (town mayor since 1954) became the first city mayor under the new charter.

---

## 9. Regional Capital and Natural Disasters (1960s–1990s)

Through the 1960s, rail and road links improved and PAL introduced jet flights on the Manila–Legazpi route. Legazpi Junior Colleges (founded 1948) became **Aquinas University of Legazpi** in 1968 under the Dominican Order, and **Bicol University** was established by Republic Act No. 5521 in 1969.

Mayon Volcano erupted violently in April–May 1968 (over 100 explosive events, six deaths), and in October 1970 **Super Typhoon Sening (Joan)** — at the time the most intense typhoon on record in the Philippines, with winds near 275 km/h — devastated the city and region, with over 700 deaths reported.

When President Ferdinand Marcos declared Martial Law in September 1972, Presidential Decree No. 1 grouped the Bicol provinces into **Region V** and designated Legazpi City its administrative center and regional capital, prompting construction of a regional government center in Barangay Rawis from 1977. The city government's own account also cites **Presidential Decree No. 125 (1973)** as declaring the Municipality of Daraga part of Legazpi City — though Daraga functions today, and is described by other sources, as its own separate municipality (see the note on discrepancies below).

Pope John Paul II celebrated Mass in downtown Legazpi on February 21, 1981, during his first papal visit to the Philippines. In September 1984, Typhoon Nitang and a Mayon eruption struck within days of each other.

Imelda Crisol Roces became the city's first female mayor in December 1991 and launched the inaugural **Ibalong Festival** in October 1992. A Mayon eruption on February 2, 1993 killed 75 people and displaced over 45,000; the province subsequently created **APSEMO** (1995), cited as the Philippines' first permanent disaster-risk-reduction office.

---

## 10. Modern Legazpi (2000s–Present)

Mayor Noel Rosal's administration (from 2001) emphasized infrastructure, tourism, and disaster-resilient planning; the Landco Business Park/Pacific Mall Legazpi opened in 2001 as Bicol's first master-planned commercial district.

In 2006, Typhoons Milenyo and Reming struck in succession; Reming triggered deadly lahars from Mayon that buried Barangay Padang and killed roughly 600 people across the province. In response, the city built **Legazpi Boulevard** as both scenic road and seawall, and by 2007 Albay had adopted a Climate Change Adaptation and Disaster Risk Reduction strategy later cited nationally as a model.

Commercial growth continued with the **Embarcadero de Legazpi** waterfront complex and IT ecozone (2009), the **Legazpi Grand Central Terminal** (2009, a Galing Pook Award winner), and the opening of major malls — Ayala Malls Legazpi (2016) and SM City Legazpi (2018, the largest mall in Bicol). The city's first public hospital, Legazpi City Hospital, opened in 2018.

Legazpi topped the National Competitiveness Council's competitiveness index for component cities in 2018 and was named the Philippine Chamber of Commerce and Industry's Most Business-Friendly City (Component City category) that same year.

The **Bicol International Airport** in Daraga opened October 7, 2021, replacing the old Legazpi domestic airport. Following the disqualification of Mayor Geraldine Rosal and her husband, then-Governor Noel Rosal, over campaign-finance violations, Alfredo Garbin Jr. served as mayor from late 2024, and **Hisham Ismail** was elected mayor in the 2025 general election, becoming the youngest chief executive in the city's history.

Having met the statutory population and income requirements for **Highly Urbanized City (HUC)** status, Legazpi is now being considered for reclassification, with officials expressing intent to pursue the process.

## A Note on Discrepancies Between the Two Sources

A few points don't fully align between the city government's account and Wikipedia's more detailed narrative:

- **Daraga's current status:** the city PDF cites a 1973 decree (PD 125) folding Daraga into Legazpi City, but Daraga is treated elsewhere (and functions today) as its own separate municipality bordering Legazpi.
- **Foundation date:** the city's seal materials list September 25, 1856 as Foundation Day, while the naming decree is dated September 22 and the town's formal inauguration was October 23.
- **Founding barangays:** the city credits Rawis, Bigaa, and Lamba as the founding barangays; the fuller account centers Binanuahan (the resettled Sawangan) as the core, with those three barrios added to it.
- **Population and land area figures** differ somewhat depending on the census year and source used (see Quick Facts).
