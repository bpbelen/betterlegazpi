Brief History of Legazpi

Explore Archival History
1587

Legazpi originated as Sawangan, a small fishing and farming settlement on Albay Gulf placed under Franciscan care.

1814

Catastrophic eruption of Mayon Volcano on February 1 destroyed Sawangan. Survivors later established Binanuahan.

1856

Officially named Legazpi by Royal Decree on September 22, 1856, in honor of Spanish conquistador Miguel López de Legazpi.

1892

Achieved first cityhood status under the Becerra Law, combining Legazpi, Albay, and Daraga.

1959

Cityhood restored permanently under Republic Act No. 2234 on June 12, 1959, serving as the official city charter.

Present

Legazpi stands as the Regional Administrative Capital of the Bicol Region (Region V) and premier tourism hub.

Regional Capital

Designated administrative hub of Bicol Region, hosting national government agencies and regional offices.

City of Fun & Adventure

Famous for Mayon Volcano's iconic majestic cone, Embarcadero waterfront, and vibrant eco-tourism.

Top Competitive City

Consistently recognized among the nation's most competitive, resilient, and business-friendly component cities.
